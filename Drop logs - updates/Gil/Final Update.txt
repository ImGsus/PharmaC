A. Introduction

In today's fast-paced healthcare environment, pharmacies play a vital role

Technology plays an important role in improving business operations, especially

The system can record medicine stocks, monitor quantities, generate notifications

web-based, authorized users can access it using computers connected to the internet
or local network.

This study aims to improve the efficiency, accuracy, and security of pharmacy
inventory management. Through automation, the pharmacy staff can reduce manual

B. Background of the Study

Technology plays an important role in improving business operations, especially
in the healthcare industry. Pharmacies manage different kinds of medicines every
day, including monitoring stocks, checking expiration dates, recording purchases,

manual recording methods such as notebooks and spreadsheets. These traditional

Old:

C. Statement of the Problem

Managing inventory effectively keeps medicine available, safe, and high-quality.
Yet, many small and medium pharmacies still rely on paper logs and simple spreadsheets
to track stock and expiration dates. These manual methods frequently lead to human
error, inaccurate records, late reorders, and expired products slipping through the

Pharmacy inventory inefficiencies cause stock issues, financial loss, and safety

A study is conducted to develop a web-based pharmacy inventory system with stock
and expiry tracking to automate workflows, improve record accuracy, and support
data-driven decisions through automated alerts and reports.

1. How can the pharmacy efficiently manage and monitor medicine inventory?
2. How can the system help pharmacy staff track medicine expiration dates?
3. How can the system provide notifications for low-stock and expired medicines?
4. How can the system enhance the accuracy, security, and reliability of pharmacy
   inventory records compared to manual methods?
5. How can the system support pharmacy management in making informed inventory
   and operational decisions?

C. Statement of the Problem

Managing pharmacy records effectively is important for maintaining organized
medicine stocks, monitoring expiration dates, and recording pharmacy transactions.
However, many small and medium pharmacies still rely on paper logs and simple
spreadsheets to track inventory, purchases, sales, and expiry dates. These manual
methods may lead to human error, inaccurate records, delayed replenishment, and
expired products being overlooked.

Pharmacy record-management problems may affect stock availability, increase
product waste, and make operational decisions more difficult. Some existing
systems may also be expensive, complex, or lacking the integrated inventory,
expiry, transaction, and reporting functions needed by smaller pharmacies.

This study aims to develop a web-based pharmacy inventory and sales management
system with stock monitoring and expiry tracking. The system is intended to
centralize pharmacy records, support transaction-triggered stock updates, identify
low-stock and expiry conditions, and provide reports for operational decision-making.

The study seeks to answer the following questions:

1. How can a web-based system help the pharmacy manage medicine, purchase,
   supplier, and sales records?
2. How can the system help authorized users monitor medicine quantities and
   expiration dates?
3. How can the system identify low-stock, out-of-stock, near-expiry, and expired
   products through alerts or reports?
4. How can the system improve the organization, security, and accessibility of
   pharmacy records compared with manual methods?
5. How can the system support pharmacy management in making informed inventory,
   purchasing, sales, and operational decisions?

Reason for update:
The revised problem statement reflects the actual modules without claiming that
the system completely eliminates all pharmacy risks.

Delimitations

a. The system supports pharmacy inventory, purchasing, sales, dispensing-related

   verified multi-branch management or external supplier, insurance, and billing

g. Prescription verification is a record and manual status workflow. The system
   does not establish that prescription approval is required before every sale.
h. Temperature monitoring is based on manually recorded readings. Sensor-based
   automatic capture is not verified.
i. Demand forecasting and reorder suggestions are rule-based calculations using
   recorded sales and stock information, not a validated predictive model.

PAGES 8-9 - I. OPERATIONAL DEFINITION OF TERMS
---------------------------------------------------------------------

Old:

Inventory Management System - A computerized system used to organize, monitor,
and control the storage and movement of medicines and pharmacy products.

Stock Monitoring - The process of tracking the quantity and availability of
medicines to avoid shortages or overstocking.

Expiry Tracking - A feature that monitors the expiration dates of medicines to
prevent the use or sale of expired products.

Web-Based System - A system that operates through a web browser and can be
accessed using the internet or a local network.

Database - An organized collection of related data used for storing medicine
records, transactions, and user information.

Administrator - The authorized user responsible for managing the system, users,
reports, and overall operations.

Pharmacist - A licensed professional or system user who manages medicine inventory,
stock updates, and expiry monitoring.

Medicine Inventory - The complete list and record of medicines available in the
pharmacy, including quantity, price, and expiration date.

Report Generation - The process of creating inventory reports, stock summaries,
and expiry reports from the system data.

User Authentication - A security process that verifies the username and password
of users before allowing access to the system.

Enhanced:

Inventory Management System - A web-based system used to organize medicine,
supplier, purchase, sales, stock, expiry, and transaction records.

Stock Monitoring - A system function that displays and checks recorded medicine
quantities, reorder levels, stock movements, and low-stock conditions.

Expiry Tracking - A system function that records and identifies medicine expiry
dates and displays expiry-related information in product and report views. It
supports the detection of expired products but does not by itself guarantee that
every expired-product sale is prevented.

Web-Based System - A system accessed through a web browser when the Laravel server
is available through the configured local network or hosted connection.

Database - An organized collection of related data used to store medicine,
purchase, sales, stock movement, supplier, user, prescription, temperature, and
audit records.

Administrator - An authorized user assigned permissions to manage configured
system functions, users, reports, and operational records.

Pharmacist - An authorized pharmacy user who may be assigned permissions to perform
configured inventory, sales, dispensing-related, or expiry-monitoring tasks. The
system does not verify a separate pharmacist role unless one is configured by the
administrator.

Medicine Inventory - The recorded list of medicines and pharmacy products,
including quantities, prices, suppliers, batch information, and expiry dates.

Report Generation - A system function that displays inventory, sales, purchase,
supplier, expiry, stock movement, valuation, batch, forecasting, and compliance
information and supports CSV export for configured reports.

User Authentication - A security process that verifies a user's login credentials
before allowing access to protected system functions.

Barcode Lookup - A system function that searches for a product using its stored
barcode through compatible scanner input or typed barcode input.

Point-of-Sale Transaction - A recorded pharmacy sales transaction containing the
selected product, quantity, price, customer or transaction information, payment
method, and authorized user details.

Reason for update:
These definitions now describe the implemented system without claiming pharmacist-
specific authorization, automatic clinical enforcement, public Internet deployment,
or guaranteed prevention of every expired-product sale.
